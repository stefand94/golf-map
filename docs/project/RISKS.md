| R-10 | Course identity is **array position**, not an id — `TRIP`, `tripDays[].items[].i` and **share-link hashes** all key off index into `C` | Any change that reorders or drops a record silently corrupts every saved trip and makes every share link already in the wild render a *different* set of courses — no error, just wrong | **Largely mitigated 2026-09-20 (GOLF-163)** | **MITIGATED `144d4ae`.** Every course now carries a stable `id`, and `data/course-ids.js` freezes the v1 `index → id` table so pre-163 share links and `localStorage` still decode after any future reorder. `scripts/test_course_ids.js` reverses `C` and asserts legacy indices still resolve to the same courses — the guarantee is now tested rather than merely respected. Residual, and it is why this row stays open: the frozen table must never be edited or lose entries, and any new merge script must still patch in place rather than rebuild. **Original framing, kept for context:** raised 2026-09-20 by the Developer session while scoping the course-data work; it is the reason that work is not a "redo".** Saved trips live in the visitor's own browser and are migratable on load. Share links are not: they are URLs already sent to other people, frozen and outside our control. Mitigation is **GOLF-163** (add a stable `id`, ship a frozen `index → id` table for the current ordering, migrate `localStorage`, emit ids in new links while still decoding old ones) — and until all four steps land, **no bulk re-pull, re-sort or record deletion may touch `data/courses-*.js`**. Verified, not assumed: `C` is never reordered or mutated in `js/` (no `sort`/`splice`/`push`/`reverse`/`filter` on it), `js/explore.js:480` sorts an array of *index values* rather than renumbering, and the merge scripts patch in place via `re.subn` per record. **But that is a property of how today's scripts are written, not of the data** — a new script that rebuilds the array from a source list re-indexes and voids all of it. Hence GOLF-161's requirement to patch line-by-line plus a test asserting `C.length` and every `C[i].n` unchanged. |
| R-11 | We publish top100golfcourses.com's rank positions against terms that explicitly bar it, by deliberate decision (DEC-022) | A takedown demand, or a rebuild under time pressure at the worst moment — i.e. at commercial launch | **Accepted — and now known-temporary** | **Upgraded 2026-09-20: the owner has confirmed revenue generation is part of the vision, so the re-open trigger is a *when*, not an *if*.** GOLF-160 is parked on that basis, deliberately rather than by neglect. **Any monetisation scope must include re-opening DEC-022.** **This is an accepted risk, not an unnoticed one.** The owner has weighed it: the site is a free hobby with no audience, the realistic worst case is a takedown email, and the fix (GOLF-160's fallback) is specced and costs hours. **The risk is not the exposure — it is the trigger being missed.** DEC-022 is explicitly coupled to the site staying non-commercial and unpublicised; the owner himself named commercialisation as the point where this stops being acceptable. **Any move to a commercial product, a custom domain with real traffic (GOLF-35 Phase B), or a public launch (GOLF-129) must re-open DEC-022 before shipping.** Secondary, and cheap to close: the permission email to Top 100 Golf Courses Ltd is unsent — a small company, a free non-commercial planner that links back, and a genuine chance of yes. **Golf Australia Magazine's terms are now read (GOLF-119 §9a) and fall in the same class** — arrangement copyright over the ranking — so the AU dataset inherits this risk rather than escaping it, and is folded into DEC-022's scope and the same permission email. AU is narrower than the UK position on one point: no sui generis database right (*IceTV*). |
| R-1 | A single free-tier provider (OpenRouteService) backs both driving directions and place search, with no fallback and no health check | A silent account- or host-side change takes a live feature down | **Materialised twice** | **Rewritten 2026-09-20 — this risk was pointed at the wrong service.** It used to say the *Geocoding* endpoint intermittently 403s. That was true in Aug 2026, but on 2026-09-19/20 the opposite happened: geocoding answered 200 while **directions** 403'd for a day (GOLF-154). Root cause was neither quota nor a bad key — ORS moved its v2 services to `api.heigit.org` and the legacy host started refusing the token; geocoding kept working because geocoding did **not** move, and still has no announced home on the new host. So the real risk is not "endpoint X is flaky", it is the single dependency plus silent failure. Diagnostic rule worth keeping: **401 means the key is unrecognised, 403 means it is recognised and refused** — check which before theorising about quota. Mitigations: GOLF-155 (log the upstream error body, so the next one explains itself), a health check, and GOLF-143 structurally. |

(The old wording said "557 courses", a stale figure; the data files hold 879 courses, 565 shown on the map.) |
