## 0. The short version

1. **The research changes who to pitch, and how.** Top 100 Golf Courses has been controlled since 2021–22 by David Davis, who also runs the **Lockhart Travel Club**, its invitation-only sister golf travel club. Lockhart runs group trips of 8–12 golfers, including Scotland in 2026. **Pitch to Davis, and pitch Lockhart as the first user.** It plans exactly the trips Golftripper plans. Lockhart trips are also the most natural "access" perk for Stefan.
2. **Don't ask for panellist status or free rounds.** Top 100's own guidance warns clubs about people who claim rater status to get complimentary golf. That ask would read as exactly that. Ask instead for things that are partnership and marketing for them: a credit, a newsletter feature, a guest place on a Lockhart or partner event, and introductions.
3. **Lead prices are lower than I said before.** Golfbreaks publicly pays **$10–20 per lead** through its US affiliate scheme. I had guessed £25–75. At that rate, £5k a year from leads means 300+ leads, which isn't realistic soon. Lead generation is a proof tool and a small earner, not the route to £5k.
4. **There's a zero-selling earner nobody had listed:** Golfbreaks' affiliate scheme on Awin, which pays per enquiry. It's a single "Get a quote" link. It fits a man with a day job better than signing operators.
5. **The space is filling up.** Birdie (Open Links Golf, launched October 2025) already offers shareable, day-by-day map itineraries. Our real edges are narrower than the pitch draft says: per-person costs across currencies, real drive and ferry times, and free editing with no login. Stefan's hands-on check of Top 100's planner, and now of Birdie too, decides the pitch.

Recommended path: open with a **6-month partner pilot (Option A)**, mostly perks with a small or zero fee. Raise **Lockhart (Option B)** in the same conversation. Fall back to a **plain link-and-credit deal (Option D)**. Aim to convert to a **paid co-branded licence (Option C)** at the pilot's end.

## 2. The BA's four questions, answered with the research

1. **Quote pilot.** Agreed, it's proof, not money. The research makes this firmer: the public price is $10–20 per lead.
   - One operator saying yes in writing is enough proof.
   - The minimum ask is an email plus a one-page terms sheet, no calls, with leads free for 3 months.
   - New: **join the Golfbreaks affiliate scheme first.** It earns per enquiry with zero selling.
   - Treat the specialist-operator pilot as optional proof for the Top 100 call.
2. **The "Top 100" flag.** It isn't zero exposure (a list compilation can carry UK database right; this is [J], not legal advice).
   - Keep it, and put display permission into every option below.
   - Don't remove it before the pitch, and don't sell it as "your brand on every pin".
   - Be able to remove it, or swap to our own criteria, within a week if asked.
   - Davis is a rater; he will notice. Raise it yourself on the call, briefly and early: "we show your list as a flag only and removed the positions; we'd like to do this properly with you". That's more credible than waiting to be caught.
3. **Proof metrics.** A Worker counter with no personal data is enough. Count:
   - trips with at least 2 days;
   - share links created;
   - share links opened.
   Put a number in the email only if it's strong. Otherwise use the demo link and one group's quote, and take the numbers to the call. 3–4 weeks of data is enough.
4. **Data and IP.**
   - The OSM coordinates are licensed under ODbL. They can be used under a licence deal (attribution, and share-alike if a derived database is published), but can't be owned outright.
   - A licence deal (no sale now) makes this simpler: we license *our* software and curation. The OSM obligations just carry through.
   - Prepare a one-page provenance note anyway. Davis will ask where the course data came from.
   - **Do the employment-contract check before the first email.** It takes 10 minutes and could change everything. Look for any clause on IP, outside business, or permission for side work.

## 3. Top 100 licence options

Common terms across all options:
- **[J] IP:** stays with Stefan; they get a licence.
- **[J] Support:** "best efforts", **no SLA** (no guaranteed response or uptime). A fix target of "within 5 working days" for anything that breaks.
- **[J] Tax:** non-cash consideration (rounds, trips, hospitality) can count as taxable income for a sole trader. Get a one-line check from an accountant.

### Option A: 6-month partner pilot (recommended opening)
- **What they get:**
  - Golftripper co-branded "with Top 100 Golf Courses" for GB&I trips.
  - An "Open in planner" link from their GB&I course pages, ranking pages and/or AI results.
  - A monthly usage summary: trips started from their links, shares, opens.
  - A newsletter story ("plan your Top 100 trip").
  - No fee.
- **What Stefan gets:**
  - **Cash:** £0, or a token £500–1k **[J]**.
  - **Non-cash, written into the agreement:**
    1. a credit and followed link, "Trip planning partner", on their travel or trip-planner page (search value);
    2. one newsletter feature at launch;
    3. permission to display Top 100 membership and positions on golftripper.uk for the term plus 6 months;
    4. a guest place at one Top 100 or Lockhart golf day in GB&I during the pilot, at cost or hosted (their choice);
    5. introductions to 3 of their resort or tour-operator advertisers.
- **Support:** about 10 hours of setup (co-brand, link format), then ≤2 hours a week, best efforts.
- **Term and exit:** 6 months. Either side can end it with 30 days' notice. Branding comes down within 14 days of ending. At month 5, both sides decide on Option C or end.
- **Rankings clause:** "Top 100 Golf Courses grants Golftripper a non-exclusive, royalty-free licence to display Top 100 membership and published positions for GB&I courses, credited 'Rankings: Top 100 Golf Courses' and linked, for the term plus 6 months. After that, Golftripper reverts to unranked display unless renewed."
- **Objections and answers:**
  - *"Why would you do it free?"* "I'd rather prove it on your audience than argue about value. If it works, we agree a licence at month 5."
  - *"Our AI planner already does this."* Show the gap side by side: their output against the same trip in Golftripper with costs per person and drive times. Only claim gaps Stefan has seen himself.
  - *"You're one person."* "It's a pilot, scoped to GB&I, with no SLA. That's why it's free."
  - *"We'll build it ourselves."* "You might. The pilot tells you whether it's worth building, at no cost."
- **[J] Opening and fallback:** open with all five perks plus £1k. Settle for perks 1–3 and £0. Perk 4 (a guest place) is the one to push for gently; drop it before dropping 1–3.

### Option B: Lockhart planning tool (raise it with Davis in the same conversation)
- **What they get:** a "Lockhart Trip Planner" instance (white label) for the club's GB&I trips. Staff build each trip; members get a share link with drive times, stays and costs. Optionally, Stefan adds the courses for a specific trip region on request.
- **What Stefan gets:**
  - **Cash:** £1.5–3k a year **[J]**.
  - **Non-cash:** one guest place a year on a Lockhart GB&I trip, on hosted or at-cost terms.
  - Fallback: £0 and a guest place, if the cash is the sticking point.
- **Support:** about 15 hours of setup (branding, subdomain), then about 2–4 hours a month. New regions are quoted per region, with 20–40 hours a region as an outline **[J]**.
- **Term and exit:** 12 months, renewing yearly, with 60 days' notice. If Stefan stops maintaining it, they keep the right to run the last delivered version for 12 months (a handover clause instead of source escrow).
- **Rankings clause:** same as A. Lockhart already uses the rankings, so no extra term is needed.
- **Objections and answers:**
  - *"Our trips are on six continents."* "Start with Scotland and Ireland. Other regions are a data job, priced separately."
  - *"Our members expect concierge, not software."* "Your concierge builds it; members get a live, shareable plan instead of a PDF."
  - *"Privacy of members."* "Trips live in the browser and the share link; there are no accounts and no member database."
- **[J] Why it matters:** this is the clearest "they'd use it every week" story. The guest place is the access perk Stefan actually wants.

### Option C: co-branded licence (the paid target at the end of the pilot)
- **What they get:**
  - "Top 100 Golf Courses Trip Planner — GB&I" on their subdomain or embedded, in their branding, with their rankings shown.
  - **Sponsored placements** they can sell to their advertisers: featured resorts or hotels in the planner, clearly labelled. They keep the ad revenue, or split it 80/20 to them.
  - Monthly usage reports.
- **What Stefan gets:**
  - **Cash:** £5k a year **[J]** (open at £8k), or £3k plus perks.
  - **Non-cash:** the credit continues, an annual guest place, and rankings display on golftripper.uk.
- **Support:** about 25 hours of setup, then 4 hours a month routine plus 20 hours a year of change requests. Anything beyond that at £60/hour **[J]**.
- **Term and exit:** 12 months, auto-renewing, with 60 days' notice. The same handover clause as B.
- **Rankings clause:** as A, for the licence term plus 6 months.
- **Objections and answers:**
  - *"Too expensive for a small site."* Fall back to £3k plus perks, or stay on A.
  - *"Who sells the sponsored placements?"* "You do; it's your ad inventory. I build the slot."
  - *"What if you get hit by a bus?"* Point to the handover clause.
- **[J] Opening and fallback:** open at £8k a year. Accept £5k, or £3k plus the guest place. Below that, stay on A's terms.

### Option D: "Open in Golftripper" link and credit (fallback)
- **What they get:** a link from their AI result or course pages that opens the trip in golftripper.uk under our brand, crediting "Rankings: Top 100 Golf Courses". Nothing for them to host.
- **What Stefan gets:** referral traffic (the most valuable thing for us at zero traffic), the rankings display permission, and a credit link. No cash.
- **Support:** about 1 hour a month.
- **Term and exit:** open-ended; either side ends it with 30 days' notice.
- **Rankings clause:** as A, while the link is live.
- **Objections:**
  - *"We'd be sending visitors away."* "Only once the AI's job is done; the share page credits you and links back."
  - If they still refuse, ask for the rankings permission alone, in exchange for credit and a link.

**Overall recommendation:** in the first call, aim for **A plus B together**, framed as one partnership with Top 100 and Lockhart. Keep **C** as the month-5 conversation and **D** as the floor. Put any concessions into perks, not cash.

### Asking for perks without looking unserious
1. **Put them in writing as deal terms**, in a numbered list next to the cash figure. Terms read as payment; casual asks read as favours.
2. **Ask for things that are marketing for them:** a credit, a newsletter feature, an event place, introductions. They cost them little and promote the partnership.
3. **Say the trade-off out loud:** "I'd take a lower fee in exchange for being involved, for example a place at one of your golf days." It's honest and shows priorities.
4. **Never ask for:** panellist status, free rounds at member clubs, or anything that looks like using their name to get on a course. Their own guidance flags exactly that.
5. **Separately, start earning the panellist route** through 25 reviews, then attend an event. It's the legitimate way to the access Stefan wants, and it builds the relationship before the pitch.
